# Product Marketing Context

**Document version:** v5
**Last updated:** 2026-11-28

## Product Overview
**One-liner:** ARVA builds the software people have been missing: simple and pleasant on the surface, carefully engineered underneath.
**What it does:** ARVA makes its own products, each for something people want and can't find, or can only find done badly. Every product hides a lot of engineering under a surface that a non-expert can use without help.
**Product category:** A maker of its own web products (not an agency, not work for hire).
**Product type:** Web apps; Solaris (in progress) is a business system for tattoo studios.
**Business model:** [NEED: how ARVA earns, per product; music, Vincent and anon are free today]

## Target Audience
Each product has its own reader. What they share: they are on the other side of an expert tool.
**Use cases:**
- music.ar-va.com: a record collector shows the collection to friends who don't collect; the friends browse covers and listen.
- vincent.ar-va.com: a Nextcloud admin who isn't a designer wants a theme in the organisation's colours that looks designed and stays readable.
- anon.ar-va.com: someone wants to say something to Artur but is afraid to say it with their name on it.
- Solaris: a tattoo studio's artists, manager and front desk want one system that does the routine work while they keep control.

## Problems & Pain Points
**Core problem:** The tool that exists was built for experts, or nobody built it.
**Why alternatives fall short (owner's words, 2026-11-26):**
- Discogs: "a nerdy Discogs website that looks old with too many details"; non-collectors "want to see pictures, and give a listen".
- Nextcloud themes: "look terrible and none of them are updated" (owner's view; not verified, so the site says what Nextcloud's own settings do instead).
- Saying something hard: people are "too afraid to do so".
**Emotional tension:** embarrassment at showing a cluttered tool; fear of being identified; losing control to software that decides for you.

## Differentiation
**Key differentiators:**
- The engineering is deep and hidden: music's record turns at 33⅓ rpm with a synthesized needle drop; Vincent's tests run about a thousand generated themes through every contrast check; the brand page's own leaf is checked on every build.
- Built for the non-expert first.
- People stay in control (their colours, public or private, their studio's decisions).

## Customer Language
**How the owner describes the products (verbatim, 2026-11-26):**
- "a wrapper for Discogs that innovates on how the vinyl collection is being shown"
- "one of a kind theme generator for Nextcloud that people have been wanting to have forever"
- "combines simplicity and openness to a touchy subject"
- "making it think and do work for them but at the same time give them the reassurance that they are still in control"
- "overengineered creative choices that make the website pleasant and fun to interact with without making them flashy or bloated"
**Words to use:** shelf, records, covers, listen; your colours, readable; say, private link; studio, control.
**Words to avoid:** heritage, generations, century-old, workshop, craft (as ARVA's past); revolutionary, seamless, unlock, solution (as a noun on its own).

## Brand Voice
| Attribute | Means | Doesn't mean | Example line |
|-----------|-------|--------------|--------------|
| Plain | Short sentences, concrete nouns | Childish or chatty | "Tell me anything" (anon) |
| Exact | Numbers and mechanisms that can be checked | Spec-sheet dumps | "Nine circles for the lobes, nine for the notches and one straight line." |
| Warm | Speaks to one person, about their thing | Cute or emoji | "Records are better shared" (login page) |
| Unhurried | Confident without selling | Vague or slow | "What was missing, made well" |

**Person:** first person singular. ARVA speaks as Artur: "I make", "my products", "tell me"; never "we", never ARVA in the third person in copy. First name only.
**Tone by context:** product pages warm and concrete; brand rules precise; errors say what happened and what to do.
**Never use (and why):** claims of age or tradition (ARVA is new); hype words (the brand rules forbid them); exclamation marks and emoji (brand rules).
